Components Fluxes and Residence Time Calculations in Global Water Budgets

Quick lookup

  • Reservoir Size: Oceans hold ~1,338,000,000 cubic kilometers of water, representing approximately 96.5 percent of all global water resources.
  • Flux Balance: Global precipitation equals global evaporation in steady-state models, calculated at ~505,000 cubic kilometers per year.
  • Residence Time Calculation: Residence time equals reservoir volume divided by the flux rate, yielding roughly 3,700 years for the ocean.
  • Atmospheric Turnover: Atmosphere holds ~12,900 cubic kilometers with a rapid flux of ~505,000 cubic kilometers per year, giving ~9 days residence.
  • Icecap Storage: Glaciers and permanent snow contain ~24,064,000 cubic kilometers, holding about 68.7 percent of Earth's total freshwater.
  • Groundwater Pool: Groundwater represents ~30.1 percent of freshwater, with deep fossil aquifers exhibiting residence times exceeding 10,000 years.

Critical facts & traps
  • Residence time = Volume / Flux rate; always ensure consistent volume and time units before dividing.
  • Oceans dominate volume (~96.5 percent) but the atmosphere dominates turnover speed (~9-day residence).
  • Trap: Confusing renewable shallow groundwater with deep fossil groundwater having multikincentury residence times.

Orographic Precipitation Mechanisms and the Rain Shadow Effect

Quick lookup

  • Lifting Mechanism: Forced ascent of moist marine air over mountain barriers causes adiabatic cooling at the moist adiabatic lapse rate.
  • Saturation Threshold: Cooling reduces air temperature to its dew point, leading to cloud formation and heavy windward orographic precipitation.
  • Adiabatic Warming: Descending air on the leeward side undergoes compression and warms at the dry adiabatic lapse rate of ~9.8 degrees Celsius per kilometer.
  • Relative Humidity Drop: Leeward warming increases saturation vapor pressure, drastically dropping relative humidity and creating an arid rain shadow.
  • Formula Application: Temperature change equals vertical displacement multiplied by lapse rate, calculated in degrees Celsius per kilometer.
  • Quantitative Example: A 2000 m rise cools air by ~13 degrees Celsius (moist rate ~6.5 degrees Celsius per kilometer), dropping vapor capacity.

Critical facts & traps
  • Windward slopes experience high precipitation and dense vegetation; leeward slopes form arid rain shadows.
  • Use moist adiabatic lapse rate (~5-6 degrees Celsius per kilometer) on the windward rising side after condensation begins.
  • Trap: Assuming dry adiabatic lapse rate below cloud base instead of the saturated/moist adiabatic lapse rate.

Hydrologic and Geomorphic Expression along the Arid-Semiarid-Humid Spectrum

Quick lookup

  • Arid Classification: Aridity index (precipitation divided by potential evapotranspiration) falls below 0.20, featuring ephemeral streams and playas.
  • Semiarid Expression: Index ranges from 0.20 to 0.50, characterized by intermittent streams, grasslands, and moderate caliche soil horizons.
  • Humid Environment: Index exceeds 0.65, dominated by perennial streams, high drainage density, and intense chemical weathering processes.
  • Evaporite Deposition: Arid closed basins concentrate dissolved ions through high evaporation, precipitating gypsum, halite, and borate minerals.
  • Stream Regimes: Ephemeral channels flow exclusively during flash floods; perennial channels maintain baseflow supplied by the local water table.
  • Geomorphic Clues: Alluvial fans, bajadas, and inselbergs dominate arid landscapes, whereas V-shaped valleys and floodplains dominate humid regions.

Critical facts & traps
  • Arid-semiarid-humid spectrum is governed by the ratio of annual precipitation to potential evapotranspiration.
  • Perennial streams intersect the water table; ephemeral streams sit above the water table and flow only post-precipitation.
  • Trap: Confusing intermittent streams (seasonal baseflow) with ephemeral streams (strictly storm-flow driven).

Calculation of Stream Sinuosity and Quantitative Channel Planform Classification

Quick lookup

  • Sinuosity Equation: Sinuosity equals channel length divided by valley length (P equals L_c divided by L_v), being dimensionless.
  • Straight Threshold: Channels with sinuosity between 1.0 and 1.05 are classified as straight channels, typically constrained by bedrock.
  • Meandering Criterion: Channels with sinuosity greater than 1.50 are classified as meandering, featuring distinct cut banks and point bars.
  • Braided Planform: Characterized by multiple active channels separated by unvegetated bars, high sediment load, and steep gradient.
  • Anastomosing Rivers: Multiple interconnected stable channels divided by heavily vegetated floodplains and low gradient settings.
  • Worked Example: A stream channel length of 12.5 km in a valley length of 8.0 km yields sinuosity equal to 12.5 divided by 8.0 equal to 1.56.

Compare / identify

Critical facts & traps
  • Sinuosity P is strictly greater than or equal to 1.0; P greater than 1.5 defines meandering channel planform.
  • Braided systems require high bedload supply and fluctuating discharge, unlike stable anastomosing channels.
  • Trap: Measuring straight-line map distance instead of valley length when computing sinuosity ratios.
